How a domain lookup works

Three steps, usually completed in under two seconds.

Clean the input

Paste a domain or full URL. We extract the hostname, lower-case it and convert international names to ASCII (Punycode).

Find the registry

The IANA directory tells us which registry runs the extension, including multi-part ones like .co.uk.

Read and display

We fetch RDAP and WHOIS records, merge them and show registrar, dates, nameservers, DNS records, status and contacts.

DNS records explained

Every domain resolves through DNS. Here is what each record type means and why it matters.

A Record

Maps a domain to an IPv4 address. The most common record — it tells browsers where your website lives.

AAAA Record

Maps a domain to an IPv6 address. The modern successor to A records, used for next-generation internet.

MX Record

Mail Exchange — tells the internet which servers handle email for your domain.

NS Record

Name Server — points to the authoritative DNS servers that hold your domain's records.

TXT Record

Holds text data for verification, SPF, DKIM and domain ownership checks.

CNAME Record

Canonical Name — aliases one domain to another, often used for subdomains.

Frequently asked questions

Is the domain data live?

Yes. Each search queries the registry's RDAP service and, where available, the WHOIS server. Results are cached for about 60 seconds to keep things fast and fair to the registries.

Why is the domain owner's name missing?

Since 2018 most registries and registrars hide personal contact details for privacy (GDPR and similar laws). DomainLookup shows only what is published and never guesses an owner.

Does "no record found" mean the domain is available?

Usually yes. When the registry confirms there is no record, we show the domain as available. Reserved and blocked names can look the same, so always confirm with a registrar before buying.

What is the difference between WHOIS and RDAP?

WHOIS is the older plain-text protocol. RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is the modern, structured replacement that returns JSON. DomainLookup queries both and shows whichever is available.

Do you store my searches?

No accounts, no cookies, no analytics and no search history. See our privacy policy.
